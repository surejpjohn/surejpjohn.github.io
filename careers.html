Get ready to apply

Quick tips to help you stand out, interview with confidence and stay safe while job hunting.

Quick tips on your CV

  • Tailor it to each job. Use the key words from the advert, since many employers screen CVs with software.
  • Show results, not duties. Start points with action verbs (led, built, analysed) and add numbers where you can.
  • Keep it short. Two pages is plenty for most graduate roles. Open with a 3–4 line profile written for the role.
  • Include all relevant experience, including projects, research, internships and volunteering.
  • Follow NZ norms. Leave out your photo, age and marital status. Check spelling, and make sure your LinkedIn matches your CV.

Quick tips for interviews

  • Research the organisation: its products, customers, values and recent news.
  • Use STAR to structure your answers: Situation, Task, Action (what you did) and Result.
  • Prepare examples that match each requirement in the job description.
  • For online interviews, test your camera, microphone and link beforehand, and log in early.
  • Ask two or three questions, for example “What does success look like in the first six months?”
  • Follow up with a short thank-you email within a day. If you’re unsuccessful, politely ask for feedback.

Avoiding job scams

Genuine employers never ask you to pay a fee to apply or to get a job. Watch for these red flags:

  • You’re offered a job without a proper interview.
  • The pay is unusually high for little work or “simple tasks” like liking posts or rating products.
  • You’re asked to pay for training, equipment, visas or a starter kit.
  • Contact happens only through WhatsApp, Telegram or a personal email address.
  • You’re pressured to decide quickly or keep the offer secret.
  • You’re asked to receive money and pass it on. This can make you a money mule.

Protect yourself: contact the company through its own website rather than the advert, check it on the NZ Companies Register, and don’t share bank details, your IRD number or passport copies until you have a verified written offer.

If something feels wrong: stop contact, call your bank if you’ve shared financial details, and report it to Netsafe or the National Cyber Security Centre. You’re also welcome to check with me or flag it in the Postgrad Careers Hub group.
